# M4 prime-square compression and its endpoint remainder

This 2026-11-26 paper calculation closes complete BBP blocks modulo p^2.
It supplies new initialized arithmetic identities, not a real visitation
estimate or a Lean theorem. The [proof plan](proof-plan.md#5-prove-the-missing-universal-visitation-theorem)
retains V. The [review record](agy-reports/m4-continuation-review.md) owns
independent review status.

Use the actual four coefficient states A, Ahat, D=A+Ahat and R=A-Ahat from
the [prime-compression assessment](m4-prime-compression.md). Write
c(j)=sum_(r=1)^7 c_r/(8j+r), P_c(z)=sum_r c_r*z^(r-1), and S_n^c for the
zero-initialized prefix, with S_(-1)^c=0. Their coefficients are dyadic.
For a prime p>=7 let q2=(2^(p-1)-1)/p, reduced modulo p when needed. Congruences
of rationals always require denominators prime to p.

## Complete regular blocks reduce to a Fermat quotient

Define the non-pole sum, including every endpoint exclusion,

    K_c(p;s)=sum_(j=0)^s sum_(r:p does not divide 8j+r)
                    c_r*16^(-j)/(8j+r) mod p,
    K_c(p)=K_c(p;p-1).

The exact complete-block values are

| State | K_c(p) modulo p |
| --- | --- |
| R | (15/4)*chi_4(p)*q2 |
| D | -(15/4)*q2 |
| A | (15/8)*(chi_4(p)-1)*q2 |
| Ahat | -(15/8)*(1+chi_4(p))*q2 |

Equivalently K_c(p)=(q2/2)*sum_r (T_p c)_r, where T_p is the established
coefficient transform. This assertion is for these four states. It needs no
n-dependent square condition; every denominator divisible by p is excluded.

Here is the finite-log proof. Work over the algebra generated by sqrt(2)
and i over the rationals integral at p. Its reduction can be a product of
fields; no field assumption is used. The eight roots beta^8=16 are distinct
units. For p>=7, 1-beta^(-p) is a unit: a zero in any residue component would
force 16=1 modulo p, which is excluded. Put

    L_p(x)=sum_(j=1)^(p-1) x^j/j
          =[1-x^p-(1-x)^p]/p mod p.

The quotient on the right divides polynomial coefficients by p before
evaluation, never inverts p. Root filtering, followed by grouping exponents
m=a*p+j, gives

    K_c(p)=(15/128)*sum_(beta^8=16)
                beta*P_c(beta)*L_p(beta^-1)/(1-beta^(-p)).

For a residue rho_c(beta) of P_c(z)/(1-z^8/16),
beta*P_c(beta)=-8*rho_c(beta). The R kernel is 8/((z-1)^2+1), with
residues -4i,+4i at 1+i,1-i. The D kernel is
4*d/dz log((2-z^2)/(z^2-2z+2)), with residues 4 at +/-sqrt(2) and -4
at 1+i,1-i. Other root contributions vanish.

For x_+=(1-i)/2 and x_-=(1+i)/2, the binomial identity and
chi_2(p)*2^((p-1)/2)=1+p*q2/2 modulo p^2 give

    L_p(x_+)=L_p(x_-)=q2/2 mod p.

Frobenius fixes or exchanges this pair according to chi_4(p). For the other
pair x=1/sqrt(2), set F_-=(1-x)^p/(1-x^p) and
F_+=(1+x)^p/(1+x^p). Both are 1 modulo p and

    F_-*F_+=1/(2^p-1)=1-2p*q2 mod p^2,
    L_p(x)/(1-x^p)+L_p(-x)/(1+x^p)=2*q2 mod p.

All these denominators are units: the paired denominator product reduces
to 1/2. The residue formula proves the R and D entries, and linearity proves
A and Ahat. These are finite algebra identities, with no real logarithm
branch or distribution assumption about q2.

## The initialized lift and cancellation

Now assume p>=7 prime and p^2>8n+7. Write n=p*h+s with 0<=s<p and set

    delta_r=p*r mod8 in {1,...,7}, b_r=(p*delta_r-r)/8,
    w_r=c_r*chi_2(p)^delta_r*2^((r-delta_r)/2),
    E_c(p,h,s)=sum_(r:b_r<=s) w_r/(8h+delta_r),
    M_c(p,s)=sum_(r:b_r<=s) w_r,
    H_c(p,s)=K_c(p;s)-(q2/2)*M_c(p,s) mod p.

The pole indices are b_r+p*t. The strict square condition makes all their
included multipliers 8t+delta_r less than p; coefficient denominators are
also p-units. Expanding the powers modulo p^2 gives

    c_r*16^(-b_r-p*t)
      =w_r*16^(-t)*(1-(p*q2/2)*(delta_r+8t)) mod p^2.

After dividing by the pole multiplier, the correction is a geometric term
-(p*q2/2)*w_r*16^(-t). Each complete regular block contributes
p*16^(-t)*K_c(p). The complete-block identity above cancels these two
corrections exactly. The only residual correction is the partial endpoint:

    p*S_n^c =16^(n-h)*(16*S_(h-1)^(T_p c)
                         +E_c(p,h,s)+p*H_c(p,s)) mod p^2. (1)

Every rational term is p-integral. H may use any representative modulo p.
At s=p-1, all offsets have entered and H=0. Thus, for k>=1 and
p^2>8kp-1,

    p*S_(kp-1)^c=16^(k*(p-1))*S_(k-1)^(T_p c) mod p^2.   (2)

At n=p>=11, the last block contains no pole and its regular term is c(0):

    p*S_p^c=16*(T_p c)(0)
              +p*(64*q2*(T_p c)(0)+c(0)) mod p^2.         (3)

This computes the residue of the actual regular cofactor. If a is the
canonical residue of p*S_p^c modulo p, subtract a and divide by p in (3).
The quotient (16*(T_p c)(0)-a)/p is p-integral and must be retained.
There is no claim about the real size of that cofactor.

The modulus is material. At p=11,k=1,

    (11*S_10^A-16^10*S_0^Ahat)/11^2 =10 mod11,
    (11*S_10^R+16^10*S_0^R)/11^2 =9 mod11.

Both differences have valuation exactly 2. The same right side in (2) cannot
be promoted uniformly to a congruence modulo p^3. No higher-order conclusion
about D is inferred from finite checks.

## The incomplete endpoint and the remaining real estimate

For a general endpoint put M=8(s+1)=a*p+b, 0<=b<p. The exact partial
root-filter normalization is

    K_c(p;s)=(1/8)*sum_beta beta*P_c(beta)*F_M(beta^-1)
            =-sum_beta rho_c(beta)*F_M(beta^-1),
    F_M(x)=(1+x^p+...+x^((a-1)*p))*L_p(x)+x^(a*p)*L_b(x),
    L_b(x)=sum_(j=1)^b x^j/j.

Empty sums are zero. The complete geometric prefactors above apply only
when M=8p. The incomplete term involves only two algebraic arguments for R
and four for D, but retains L_b. As s ranges below p-1, its cutoff b ranges
over every nonzero residue modulo p.

When p>8s+7, no pole has entered and

    H_c(p,s)=sum_(j=0)^s16^(-j)*c(j)=16^(-s)*S_s^c mod p. (4)

This is literally an initialized prefix, not data determined only by h.
At p=17 for R, the endpoints s=0 and s=1 have no entered poles and both
floor(8(s+1)/p)=0, yet H_R(17,0)=14 and H_R(17,1)=0. Dropping H, or
using just those two coarse endpoint summaries, is therefore invalid.
This is not a complexity lower bound for all endpoint representations.
Complementary sums and special cutoffs may simplify further.

The [finite entry countertest](m4-generating-functions.md#finite-entry-data-still-allow-a-different-avoiding-block)
also tolerates this added precision. At N=p its perturbation is
delta=16^N*t/Q. Requiring t to be divisible by p preserves p*S_N modulo p^2.
A fixed number of further digits costs a fixed power of p in the lattice
spacing. That is exp(O(log N)), while the available interval has length
exp((6-2*log16)*N+o(N)); the same denominator sieve still works. The altered
entry loses global zero initialization and supplies no infinite counterexample.

The complete-block lift is a positive arithmetic result. Its missing consumer
is a bound on the complete initialized real CRT phase, with all prime-power
components and their dependence retained. Neither a fixed extra p-adic digit
nor a presumed random Fermat quotient provides that estimate. V stays open.

## Two-anchor reconstruction from many prime endpoints

The complete-block identity has a useful joint-in-index consequence. Define
the explicit odd clearing integer

    L_N=lcm{2j+1,4j+3,8j+1,8j+5 : 0<=j<=N}.

This is the lcm of the individual factors. It clears every A(j), hence S_N,
because

    A(j)=4/(8j+1)-1/(8j+5)-(3j+2)/((2j+1)(4j+3))

and the last two factors are coprime. The odd prime support is exactly
p=1 mod4 with p<=8N+5, and p=3 mod4 with p<=4N+3. For the upper bound,
a prime 3 mod4 dividing an integer 1 mod4 has cofactor at least 3.
For the lower bound each eligible prime itself occurs in the listed factors.
Higher prime powers have size at most 8N+5 and contribute
O(sqrt(N)*log N). The already assessed fixed-progression PNT gives
log L_N=6N+o(N); no reduced-denominator survival theorem is needed.

For N>=10 put

    P_N=product_(N<p<=6N, p prime) p,
    G_N=gcd(P_N,L_N), M_N=P_N*G_N.

For a rational entry x, define x_0=x and
x_(j+1)=16*x_j+A(N+j+1). Impose the finite initialized constraints

    p*x_(p-1-N)=16^(p-1)*a_p mod p^2, for every N<p<=6N,
    a_p=47/15 if p=1 mod4, and -65/21 if p=3 mod4.       (5)

These are the exact anchors A(0) and Ahat(0). They are not values of a
separately altered low orbit. Every endpoint p-1 lies in [N,6N-1], and
p>=11 gives p^2>8p-1 and p^2>8N+5. Thus v_p(L_N) is 0 or 1, and
all actual congruence sides are p-integral. The actual S_N satisfies (5)
by the complete-block formula with k=1.

Assume L_N*x is integral and set delta=x-S_N. Exact future forcing gives
x_j-S_(N+j)=16^j*delta. Subtraction in (5) is therefore equivalent to
v_p(delta)>=1 at each selected prime. Writing a=L_N*delta in Z, this says
p^(1+v_p(L_N)) divides a. Consequently the full set of constraints is
equivalent to

    x-S_N belongs to (M_N/L_N)*Z.                       (6)

The converse also preserves the rational congruence domains. Hence the
explicit finite criterion

    M_N >= 4*16^N*L_N                                  (7)

makes S_N the unique such x in [0,4*16^N]. The actual state is strictly
inside this interval: A(j)>0, A(0)=47/15 and A(j)<4/9 for j>=1 give
0<S_N/16^N<=427/135<4. This height bound uses no pi digit.

The fixed-modulus PNT gives log P_N=5N+o(N) and log G_N=4N+o(N):
the latter combines primes 1 mod4 in (N,6N] and 3 mod4 in (N,4N+3].
Thus log(M_N/L_N)=3N+o(N), and 3>log16 makes (7) true for every
sufficiently large N. This proves an eventual arithmetic reconstruction
using only two anchors, explicit forcing, a denominator cap and a real
height bound. Neither the actual integer part nor a dyadic residue is an
assumption. No tested N is asserted to be the universal threshold.

The combined numerator modulus has logarithm 9N+o(N); the allowed numerator
height has logarithm (6+log16)N+o(N). This is why these many cross-index
constraints eliminate the former finite-entry perturbations, while a fixed
number of additional digits at one prime did not. The CRT residues still
contain the actual future forcing sums. Uniqueness does not locate the
fractional part of their canonical representative.

An actual countertest isolates that last gap. For D=A+Ahat, use
L^D_N=lcm{odd m:1<=m<=8N+7} and P^D_N=product_(N<p<=7N)p.
The lcm clears both forcings; the two half-denominator terms combine to
an integer after multiplication by the odd lcm. Its initialized constraints
have the same form, with the sole anchor D(0)=4/105. They yield

    z-U_N belongs to ((P^D_N)^2/L^D_N)*Z.

This spacing has logarithm 4N+o(N), so the same natural-height interval
eventually reconstructs U_N uniquely. Yet 0<U_N<1/(16N+18): its fractional
values always miss (1/2,1). Reconstruction alone cannot imply visitation.
D has a different forcing and zero total period; this does not refute an
additional A-specific estimate or V.

The first missing consumer is a bound showing that the reconstructed A
state leaves every proper word-avoidance set. Counting at most one bounded
CRT solution does not show that it lies outside the avoidance set. The
[exact Fourier consumer](m4-crt-phase.md) retains that selected class and
identifies what is lost by taking absolute values.

## Unrestricted differences and multiplicative indices

The following 2026-11-26 extension removes the square restriction for complete
blocks. Its conclusion is about a **difference**, whose two raw terms can each
have a p-power denominator. For every prime p>=7, k>=1, and
c in {A,Ahat,D,R},

    v_p(p*S_(pk-1)^c-16^(k*(p-1))*S_(k-1)^(T_p c))>=2. (6)

It is invalid to reduce the two terms separately in Z/p^2 Z when they are
not p-integral. Use v_p(0)=infinity. This extension is paper mathematics,
with independent review recorded separately; it is not a Lean declaration.

Write F_c(k)=sum_(j<k)16^(-j)c(j), F_c(0)=0. For k>=1,
F_c(k)=16^(-(k-1))*S_(k-1)^c. The earlier complete regular-block calculation
has no square restriction and gives

    K_c(p)-(q2/2)*sum_r(T_p c)_r in p*Z_(p).

The missing ingredient was division by a pole multiplier which may itself
be divisible by p. Put B=chi_2(p)*2^((p-1)/2) and d=B-1. Then
d in p*Z_(p), and d=p*q2/2 modulo p^2. For every positive integer m,

    (B^m-1)/m-d
      =sum_(j=2)^m binom(m-1,j-1)*d^j/j in p^2*Z_(p).

Indeed j-v_p(j)>=2 for odd p and j>=2. This accounts for the division by
m without assuming it is a unit. Multiplying by -B^(-m) also gives

    (B^(-m)-1)/m=-d modulo p^2 in Z_(p).                (7)

For the earlier pole locations b_r, transformed offsets delta_r and weights
w_r, the poles among 0<=j<pk are exactly j=b_r+p*t, 0<=t<k. With
m=8t+delta_r, the weight identity is exact:

    c_r*16^(-b_r-p*t)=w_r*16^(-t)*B^(-m).

Thus subtracting the transformed pole term in
p*F_c(pk)-F_(T_p c)(k) gives
w_r*16^(-t)*(B^(-m)-1)/m. By (7), the sum of all these differences is
-d*sum_(t<k)16^(-t)*sum_r w_r modulo p^2. The regular terms, grouped into
k blocks of length p, contribute p*K_c(p)*sum_(t<k)16^(-t) modulo p^2.
The complete-block identity cancels these contributions. Consequently

    E_p^c(k):=p*F_c(pk)-F_(T_p c)(k) in p^2*Z_(p)       (8)

for every k>=1. Multiplying by the p-unit 16^(pk-1) proves (6). Every
sum is finite; cancellation happens before taking residues of any pole terms.
At p=7,k=1,c=A, both raw terms in (6) have valuation -1 and their difference
has valuation 2. The same nonintegrality occurs at p=7,k=11.

An integral restatement retains the scaling cost. Choose e>=0 with
8k-1<p^(e+1). All denominators in S_(k-1) have valuation at most e, and
those in S_(pk-1) at most e+1. Therefore

    p^(e+1)*S_(pk-1)^c
      =16^(k*(p-1))*p^e*S_(k-1)^(T_p c) mod p^(e+2).

Both terms are now p-integral. This scales the difference and its modulus;
it does not raise the raw precision in (6). The p=11,k=1 counterexamples
to an unchanged raw modulus p^3 for A and R remain valid.

## Twisted prime-power towers

For p>=11 and r>=0 put

    Z_r^c=p^r*F_c(p^r), W_r^c=Z_r^(T_p^r c).

Here Z_0^c=c(0), not the empty prefix. The integral restatement with
k=p^(r-1), e=r-1 proves p-integrality of adjacent states for r>=1.
Equation (8) and T_p^2=identity give

    Z_r^c-Z_(r-1)^(T_p c) in p^(r+1)*Z_p,
    W_r^c-W_(r-1)^c in p^(r+1)*Z_p.

Telescoping gives W_s^c-W_r^c in p^(r+2)*Z_p for s>r. Completeness
therefore gives a limit W_infinity^c in Z_p with

    W_infinity^c-W_r^c in p^(r+2)*Z_p,
    W_infinity^c=c(0) mod p^2.

These claims about the p-adic completion are paper consequences, not newly
checked library results. For p=3 mod4 the twist is essential: the untwisted
A tower has two parity limits differing by R(0)=218/35 modulo p^2. For
p>=11 in that class this is a p-unit, so the limits differ even modulo p.
D needs no twist; R requires (-1)^r in that class. At p=7, the same
difference estimates give a twisted Cauchy tower in Q_7, but Ahat(0)=-65/21
is not integral. Multiplication by 7 gives an integral version. The main
Z_p assertion deliberately retains p>=11.

The null state illustrates the real limitation particularly sharply. Its
actual bound 0<U_n<1/(16n+18) gives

    0<Z_r^D<16^(-p^r)

in the real absolute value, while its p-adic limit is nonzero and congruent
to 4/105 modulo p^2 for p>=11. The two limits do not contradict each other,
and U_n still misses (1/2,1) for every n.

## Mixed-prime relations and the remaining consumer

For distinct primes p,q>=7 and k>=1, the transforms commute. The complete
four-term expression is

    C_(p,q)^c(k)=pq*F_c(pqk)-q*F_(T_p c)(qk)
                   -p*F_(T_q c)(pk)+F_(T_p T_q c)(k)
      =q*E_p^c(qk)-E_p^(T_q c)(k)
      =p*E_q^c(pk)-E_q^(T_p c)(k).

It belongs to both p^2*Z_(p) and q^2*Z_(q), even when individual terms
have poles. The old square restrictions at the common high index would
require incompatible inequalities of sizes p>8qk and q>8pk. The
unrestricted difference removes that obstruction.

The middle terms are essential. At p=11,q=13,k=1,c=A, the full difference
has valuations (2,2), whereas pq*F_A(pq)-F_(T_p T_q A)(1) has valuations
(-1,-1). There is no two-term composite lift obtained by discarding them.
Multiplying the commuting operators over s distinct primes gives an
inclusion-exclusion difference divisible by each selected prime square in
its local ring. It retains 2^s corners and a largest prefix length equal
to k times the product of the primes. No helpful height bound, independence
or real cancellation follows from that divisibility alone.

Finite exact checks suggest the stronger bounds

    v_p(E_p^c(k))>=2+v_p(k), c=A,Ahat,R,
    v_p(E_p^D(k))>=3+2*v_p(k).

These are **unproved diagnostics**. In particular the bound r+1 for a
normalized tower increment is not asserted to be sharp. The uniform raw
bound 2 is sharp over all k for A and R. The next bounded arithmetic
question is a proof or counterexample for these k-dependent bounds; the
separate real question is whether the mixed relations constrain the actual
forbidden-word model when every intermediate state and height cost is kept.
Neither is a visitation premise which may be silently assumed.
